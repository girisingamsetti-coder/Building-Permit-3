1.  Purpose and how to use this document
This document describes, for each stakeholder, what they need to do in the building permission portal, in what order, and what the screen should do to make that easy. Every workflow has a flowchart and a step table. The screens named here exist as artboards in the clickable prototype. Sections 13 and 14 list every screen with its fields, actions and next step.
It is written for the product owner, the UX team and the developers building the 2D drawing scrutiny flow. It sits alongside three other documents: the public 2D submission standard (what applicants must follow), the implementation guide (how the ACadSharp validation engine is built) and the SOP.
Note: Portal observations come from the public BBAS home page and public coverage of BuildNow. No logged-in screens were reviewed, so pain points below are hypotheses to test with real users. Anything shown as [in brackets] is a placeholder for a value CRDA must supply: thresholds, SLA days, department names.
Scope of this version
Amaravati Capital City area only, under the Zoning Regulations of February 2016 (with later amendments to be confirmed). The wider Capital Region is not covered.
Permits are Development Permission Orders (DPO) issued by the Commissioner, with the 60-day decision limit, followed by commencement, completion and occupancy.
R1 village plots have their own screens and rules: abutting road, road handover with TDR, and the exemption for small plots.
Fees follow the regulations (basis, exemptions, impact fee, mortgage). Rates are placeholders until CRDA supplies the notified schedule.
Section 15 gives the detail, with regulation numbers, and lists what CRDA must confirm.
2.  What we looked at
BBAS (current APCRDA portal)
The home page is a set of link lists grouped by user type: Citizen Service (pay fees, search status), Developer and TPAs (registration, renewal, consent links), and LTP (registration, renewal, consent link, view).
BBAS is built around a single 3D BIM model (architectural, structural and MEP) that is scrutinised and tracked; fire, high-rise and environmental NOCs are integrated in the same system.
Help lives in downloads: manuals, a modelling guide video, do's and don'ts and Revit plugins. The helpdesk entry is marked as coming soon.
The page offers screen-reader access and text-size controls, and the header is bilingual (English and Telugu).
BuildNow (Telangana)
The applicant's category depends on plot size and building details: registration only, instant approval, or single window. The portal shows which applies before the applicant starts.
Small residential projects can be approved on self-certification, with an official follow-up check afterwards.
Land ownership and survey details are verified automatically against land-records systems.
Officials get an operational dashboard, and applicants get an AI policy assistant for rules questions.
What this means for a 2D flow
Your workflow is 2D DWG/DXF, not BIM, so BBAS's modelling guidance does not carry over directly. The transferable ideas are: choose the route first, show one tracker to everyone, check the file at upload time, pre-fill from official records, and let officials work on exceptions instead of re-checking everything.
3.  Design principles

What it means on screen
Route first
Ask three questions, then show the route, documents, fees and timeline before the applicant uploads anything.
One tracker for all
Applicant, LTP, officer and commissioner see the same seven stages and the same clock.
Fix it before it is filed
The drawing check runs at upload. Problems appear on the drawing itself, not in a rejection letter.
Exceptions only
Officials see what needs a decision. Everything the system verified is collapsed.
Never a dead end
Every failed or returned step sits beside a next action, a deadline and a person to contact.
Plain words
Sentence case, verbs first, dates in full (Oct 3, 2026), Telugu and English side by side.
Status is never colour alone
Every status has an icon and a word. Works for colour-blind users and in print.
Phone friendly
Applicants track, pay and reply from a phone. Drawing upload stays on desktop.

Rules that apply everywhere
One clock per stage. A query pauses the clock for that stage only and tells the applicant exactly what to send.
Every action is written to an audit log with who, what, when and the file revision.
A resubmitted drawing is a new revision (R0, R1, R2). The exception report keeps history across revisions.
Notifications go by SMS, email and in-app, and always link to the exact screen where the action happens.
Rule changes apply to new submissions only. Files in progress keep the standards version they started with.
All objections are given in one go. After the applicant complies, new objections are not normally raised (Reg 212.3), so the officer step collects every exception in one decision.

14.  Screen specifications by stakeholder
Each screen starts with a simple page layout drawn from its field list, followed by the fields, the actions available and what happens next. In the layouts, a red star marks a required field and multi-page tasks show their steps across the top. Field types describe the control the user sees. 'Required' means the user cannot move on without it. Values in [brackets] are set by CRDA.
Note: Rules such as file size limits, thresholds and SLA days are placeholders. Field lists are a starting point for review with each office, not a final data dictionary.

15.  Capital City scope, zoning, R1 and fees
This section applies the Zoning Regulations of Amaravati Capital City (February 2016) to the portal design. Regulation numbers are given so each rule can be traced. Rates and table values that were not available in public sources are shown as placeholders.
15.1  Scope
Item
Decision in this version
In scope
Buildings in the Amaravati Capital City area: Development Permission Order (DPO), commencement notice and line-out check, inspections, completion and occupancy certificate, conditional uses, variances and appeals.
Out of scope
The wider APCRDA jurisdiction outside the Capital City area, which is not covered by these zoning regulations. Layout and sub-division approval is a separate process.
To confirm
Whether the Amaravati Government Complex is in scope (it has its own 2024 zoning regulations and urban design guidelines). Whether later amendments apply, including the environment-clearance gazette amendment and the R5 affordable housing zone.
Precedence
Urban design guidelines approved by the Commissioner take precedence over the zoning regulations (Reg 305.2). Where Appendix II and III differ, Appendix II prevails (Reg 304.1). The more restrictive rule governs where sections differ (Reg 101.3).
